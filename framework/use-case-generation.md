# Use-case generation — research proposals, evidence-gated

This note sits alongside `scorecard.md`. It covers the step between
scoring a sector and scoring a specific business: turning a sector into
a small set of research proposals, each with kill criteria, so that weak
ideas are killed by evidence before anyone acts on them.

Research only, not investment advice. No market size is stated here and
none should be invented in a proposal — a figure without a source and a
period is TO VERIFY, not a market.

## Sectors stay alphabetical, not ranked

The sector files in `sectors/` are starting points, in alphabetical
order. This note does not rank them, and a proposal must not imply a
ranking either: do not write "the best sector" or order proposals as if
the order were a verdict. Ranking happens only through the scorecard,
only for opportunities with evidence quality ≥ 3, and only with the
evidence attached.

## From sector to proposal (3 per sector, maximum)

For each sector under consideration, write at most 3 specific proposals
in this form — specific, not a sector restated:

- **Proposal:** "X service/product for Y customer at Z price" — with Y
  and Z named, or marked TO VERIFY if not yet known.
- **Who pays, in NPR, how often:** named payer and payment pattern, or
  TO VERIFY.
- **Nepal advantage claimed:** one sentence, tied to a structural
  reason (labour, location, resource, language, diaspora, regulation)
  — or TO VERIFY.
- **First verification step:** one concrete action (for example,
  interview a named customer group, or obtain a written licence
  requirement from the regulator).

A proposal that cannot name its customer is not a proposal yet — it is
a research task. Send it back to the "research first" list.

## Kill criteria — write them before you score

Every proposal carries kill criteria agreed in advance. If a criterion
is met, the proposal is killed (or returned to research) — it is not
rescued by enthusiasm. Examples, to be adapted per proposal:

- **No payer found:** no named customer group confirms it would pay
  the stated price, after a genuine attempt to find one.
- **Evidence quality stuck below 3:** after the first verification
  step, every score still rests on anecdote or guess.
- **Licence or regulatory blocker in writing:** the regulator's
  written requirement rules the model out as proposed.
- **Unit economics fail on real prices:** with supplier prices and
  costs actually obtained (not from articles), the margin does not
  cover working capital and payback within the proposal's own terms.
- **No moat:** nothing stops the next entrant copying it in 6 months,
  and no barrier can be evidenced.

Record which criterion killed a proposal, and what evidence did it.
A killed proposal is a result, not a failure to hide — it stops the
next person spending the same effort on the same dead end.

## Evidence gate — before any proposal is scored

A proposal may be scored on `scorecard.md` only when:

- [ ] Its customer, payer, and price are named or explicitly TO VERIFY
      per line (no line left silently blank).
- [ ] Its kill criteria are written down.
- [ ] Its first verification step has been attempted, and the result
      recorded — including a negative result.
- [ ] No market size, price, or cost appears without a source and a
      period, or a TO VERIFY label.

Proposals that fail this gate stay in research. They are not scored,
not ranked, and not presented as candidates — however promising they
sound.
