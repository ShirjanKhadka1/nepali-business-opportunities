## Verified facts

1. **Scale of inflows, FY 2024/25 (10 months to mid-May 2025).** Remittance
   inflows rose 13.2% to Rs 1,356.61 billion in the ten months of FY 2024/25,
   compared with 16.9% growth in the same period of the previous year, as stated
   by Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) in its report "Current Macroeconomic and Financial
   Situation of Nepal (Based on Ten Months Data Ending Mid-May 2024/25)".
   Source: Radio Nepal reporting of the NRB report, published 2025-06-10 —
   https://radionepalonline.com/en/2025/06/10/410704.html — accessed 2026-10-29.
2. **Single-month inflow.** In mid-April to mid-May 2025 (Baisakh 2082),
   remittance inflows stood at Rs 165.30 billion, versus Rs 115.99 billion in the
   same period of the previous year; in US dollar terms inflows rose 10.5% to
   USD 9.96 billion over the ten-month period.
   Source: same Radio Nepal / NRB report page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
3. **Migrant flow behind the inflows.** In the same ten-month period, 405,610
   Nepali workers took first-time approval for foreign employment and 280,314
   took renewal approval, versus 373,307 and 236,398 respectively a year earlier.
   Source: same Radio Nepal / NRB report page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
4. **Share of the economy.** Personal remittances were 25.99% of GDP in 2024,
   down from 26.22% in 2023; the World Bank is the stated source of the series.
   Source: TheGlobalEconomy, "Nepal Remittances, percent of GDP" (World Bank data) —
   https://pl.theglobaleconomy.com/Nepal/remittances_percent_GDP/ — accessed 2026-10-29.
5. **External-balance context.** The current account remained in surplus at
   Rs 255.93 billion in the ten months (versus Rs 193.31 billion a year earlier),
   and gross foreign exchange reserves rose 23.1% to Rs 2,512.95 billion by
   mid-May 2025 from Rs 2,041.10 billion in mid-July 2024.
   Source: same Radio Nepal / NRB report page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
6. **Regulatory barrier — licensed firms fell sharply.** In 2075/76, 52
   remittance companies were operational; by 2081 Asar only 24 remained licensed,
   with two further licences expired and unrenewed. The same page attributes
   closures in part to NRB mandatory capital hikes, with paid-up capital rising
   from Rs 2 crore toward Rs 10 crore, and reports the phased schedule as
   Rs 3 crore by 2081 Asar, Rs 4 crore by 2082 Asar, Rs 6 crore by 2083 Asar,
   Rs 8 crore by 2084 Asar and Rs 10 crore by 2085 Asar.
   Source: Fiscal Nepal reporting NRB / Nepal Money Transfer Association material —
   https://www.fiscalnepal.com/2025/06/16/20744/remittance-inflows-surge-to-rs-13-56-tn-but-remit-companies-decline-amid-capital-rules/ — accessed 2026-10-29.

## Source discrepancy — read this before reusing fact 6's page

The Fiscal Nepal page in fact 6 headlines its monetary totals in trillions
(e.g. "Rs 13.56 trillion" over ten months). Those headline figures are 10x the
NRB figures reported by Radio Nepal for the same period (Rs 1,356.61 billion =
Rs 1.356 trillion; Baisakh Rs 165.30 billion, not Rs 1.65 trillion). This dossier
therefore uses the Radio Nepal / NRB billion figures for all money amounts and
uses the Fiscal Nepal page ONLY for the licensed-company count and the capital
schedule. Do not quote the Fiscal Nepal headline trillion figures.
