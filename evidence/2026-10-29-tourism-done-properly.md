## Verified facts

1. **Arrivals, 2024.** Nepal received 1,147,567 foreign tourists in 2024,
   including 92,034 in December, according to the Nepal Tourism Board (NTB) —
   growth of nearly 13.1% on the previous year.
   Source: Free Online Library republication of the NTB-based report —
   https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Nepal%27s+tourism+sector+sees+notable+recovery+with+13%25+growth+in+2024.-a0822269199 — accessed 2026-10-29.
2. **Arrivals, 2023, for comparison.** A total of 1,014,882 foreign tourists
   visited Nepal in 2023 (same NTB-based page).
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
3. **Source markets, 2024.** The largest source was India (317,772, down 0.7% on
   2023), followed by the USA (111,216, up 10.8%) and China (101,879); the UK
   contributed 57,554, Bangladesh 48,848 and Australia 43,980. Total European
   arrivals were 206,841, up 10.4%.
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
4. **Seasonality, 2024 (monthly arrivals).** March was highest at 128,167 and
   October second at 124,393; the quietest month listed was July at 64,599.
   January 79,100; February 97,426; April 111,376; May 90,211; June 76,736;
   August 72,719; September 96,305; November 114,501.
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
5. **Recovery level.** NTB's CEO is reported as putting 2024 at 96% recovery of
   pre-pandemic visitor levels; the 2019 pre-pandemic comparator on the same page
   is 1.197 million foreign tourists (2019/20 fiscal-year framing on that page).
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
6. **Nature-based advantage and its concentration risk.** More than 45% of
   tourists to Nepal visit its natural resources; assets are managed within 20
   protected areas including 12 national parks. Two-thirds of tourists visit
   protected areas but are concentrated in only four of the 20 protected areas.
   Source: World Bank feature, "Harnessing Tourism to Enhance the Value of
   Biodiversity and Promote Conservation in Nepal" —
   https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2022/06/03/harnessing-tourism-to-enhance-the-value-of-biodiversity-and-promote-conservation-in-nepal — accessed 2026-10-29.
7. **Economic weight and jobs (2019 baseline — dated, see note).** The tourism
   industry contributed about 6.7% of Nepal's GDP with a total impact of
   US$ 2.2 billion, and in 2019 supported over one million direct and indirect
   jobs, or 6.7% of total employment, with about 80% of those jobs in the most
   remote and resource-constrained regions.
   Source: same World Bank page as fact 6 — accessed 2026-10-29.
8. **Value warning (2019 baseline — dated).** Average spend was US$ 48 per day
   per international visitor in 2019 — described on the page as almost half the
   global average and one-third of Thailand's and India's — and the page warns
   of a risk of becoming a low-value mass-tourism destination.
   Source: same World Bank page as fact 6 — accessed 2026-10-29.

## Dating note

Facts 6–8 come from a World Bank feature last updated 2022-11-24 and built on a
2019 baseline. They evidence the structural nature-based advantage and the
low-spend risk, but they are NOT current (2024/25) spend, jobs or GDP-share
figures. Do not quote them as current without a newer verified source.
