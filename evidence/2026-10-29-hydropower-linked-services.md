## Verified facts

1. **Installed capacity and mix.** As of the current fiscal year reported in the
   Economic Survey 2082/83, Nepal's total installed electricity generation
   capacity reached 4,105 MW, of which hydropower was 3,798 MW, solar 142 MW,
   alternative energy (AEPC-supported) 106.24 MW, thermal 53.4 MW and
   cogeneration (sugar mills) 6 MW.
   Source: Fiscal Nepal reporting the Economic Survey 2082/83 —
   https://www.fiscalnepal.com/2026/05/27/26228/nepal-electricity-access-reaches-nearly-100-percent-installed-capacity-hits-4105-mw/ — accessed 2026-10-29.
2. **Who builds/owns the capacity.** Of capacity integrated into the national
   grid: Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) projects 661.57 MW, NEA subsidiary
   companies 748.4 MW, and private-sector projects 2,587 MW; by Falgun 2082 a
   total of 3,997 MW had been connected to the grid.
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
3. **Access and customers.** Electricity access reached 99.1% of the population
   under the same Economic Survey reporting, with customers rising from 5.708
   million (Asar 2082) to 5.859 million (Falgun 2082).
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
4. **Exports (volume).** Nepal exported 2,918 GWh of electricity to India during
   the review period up to Falgun 2082, driven by monsoon hydropower production
   and improved cross-border transmission capacity.
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
5. **Transmission build-out.** Transmission lines of 66 kV and above totalled
   7,280 circuit km (up from 7,161 km) and 33 kV lines 8,225 circuit km;
   400/230-volt distribution lines totalled 173,043 circuit km (up from
   156,790 km).
   Source: same page as fact 1 — accessed 2026-10-29.
6. **Export earnings, FY 2024/25.** In the last fiscal year 2024–25, Nepal earned
   NPR 17.5 billion from electricity exports, according to NEA data as reported
   by IANS.
   Source: IANS report via hi INDiA —
   https://hiindia.com/nepal-earns-rs-9-37-billion-from-electricity-export-to-india-and-bangladesh/ — accessed 2026-10-29.
7. **Export route and approvals.** Nepal sells surplus electricity to India
   mainly through the Indian Energy Exchange (IEX) Day-Ahead and Real-Time
   Markets and under bilateral medium-term agreements with Haryana and Bihar,
   plus 40 MW daily to Bangladesh using India's transmission infrastructure;
   NEA had approval to export a total of 1,165 MW to India and Bangladesh
   combined. Exports typically run late May to mid-November; Nepal began
   exporting to India in November 2021.
   Source: same page as fact 6 — accessed 2026-10-29.
8. **Seasonality and disruption risk.** The same IANS/NEA report notes 32
   hydropower projects affected by floods, including 17 operating projects
   (180 MW combined) and 15 under-construction projects (338 MW combined),
   while NEA stated export supply was not affected. Separately, the report
   notes a long-term power trade agreement signed in January 2024 under which
   India committed to buying 10,000 MW from Nepal over ten years, and a Nepal
   aim to produce 28,500 MW by 2035 and export 15,000 MW.
   Source: same page as fact 6 — accessed 2026-10-29.
